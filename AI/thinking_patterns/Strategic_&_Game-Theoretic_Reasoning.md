## Section 9: Strategic & Game-Theoretic Reasoning

| Thinking Pattern | Category | Where It Is Used | Short Explanation |
|------------------|----------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Minimax Reasoning** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Chess, Go, competitive environments | Assumes an adversary will make the best possible move, and plans the strategy that minimizes the maximum possible loss |
| **Nash Equilibrium Seeking** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Negotiation bots, auctions, pricing algorithms | Finding strategies where no agent can benefit by unilaterally changing their approach when others' strategies are fixed |
| **Subgame Perfect Equilibrium** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Multi-stage dynamic games, sequential decisions | Planning that remains optimal not just overall, but from any point (subgame) onward in the interaction |
| **Backward Induction** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Finite extensive-form games, finite turn planning | Starts from the final desired (or forced) outcome and reasons backwards in time to determine the optimal first step |
| **Zero-Sum Adversarial Planning** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Cyber-security red/blue teaming, strictly competitive sports | Reasoning under the strict assumption that one agent's gain is exactly another agent's loss |
| **Coalition & Cooperative Game Theory** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Multi-agent collaboration, resource sharing | Formulating agreements where agents team up to achieve higher payouts and then divide the surplus equitably |
| **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)** | Game-theoretic reasoning | AlphaGo, large state-space planning | Probabilistically simulates many random paths to the end of a game to evaluate the strength of the current moves |
| **Signaling & Deceptive Reasoning** | Game-theoretic reasoning | Poker bots, imperfect information games | Intentionally taking actions that hide true intent or simulate weakness/strength to mislead adversaries |
